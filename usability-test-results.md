# Usability Test Results

Project: healthcare.gammal.tech  
Test Type: Mobile UX Usability Testing  
Screens Tested:
- Home / Health Dashboard
- Doctor Booking
- Profile

---

# Tester 1

Age: 23  
Background: Computer Science Student

## Task 1:
“How would you check your health score?”

### Result
The tester opened the Home screen first and looked for a health score card or circular progress indicator.

### Confusion
- The tester expected to see a visible “Health Score” number immediately on the dashboard.
- The current Home screen focused more on doctors and AI articles.
- The tester thought the “Reports” tab might contain the health score.

### Feedback
> “I expected something like 82/100 on the home page.”

---

## Task 2:
“How would you book a doctor?”

### Result
The tester successfully clicked on “Top Doctors” and understood the flow immediately.

### Confusion
- The tester expected a search filter for specialty selection.
- The appointment screen was easy to understand.

### Feedback
> “Booking was simple and clean.”

---

# Tester 2

Age: 29  
Background: Office Employee

## Task 1:
“How would you check your health score?”

### Result
The tester clicked on the Profile tab first.

### Confusion
- The tester confused calories and heart rate statistics with the overall health score.
- No dedicated health score section existed.

### Feedback
> “I can see health stats, but not my total health condition.”

---

## Task 2:
“How would you book a doctor?”

### Result
The tester tapped “Top Doctors” from the Home screen and reached the doctor details screen successfully.

### Confusion
- The tester wanted a clearer “Book Now” button earlier in the flow.
- They also expected doctor categories like:
  - Cardiologist
  - Dentist
  - Nutritionist

### Feedback
> “The appointment page looks professional.”

---

# Tester 3

Age: 35  
Background: Family Member (Non-technical)

## Task 1:
“How would you check your health score?”

### Result
The tester spent several seconds searching the Home page.

### Confusion
- Could not identify where the health score existed.
- Thought AI Warning cards were advertisements or articles instead of health insights.
- Bottom navigation icons were understandable.

### Feedback
> “The design is beautiful but I need clearer labels.”

---

## Task 2:
“How would you book a doctor?”

### Result
The tester successfully navigated to doctor booking without help.

### Confusion
- The tester wanted doctor ratings to appear larger.
- The date selector was slightly small for quick tapping.

### Feedback
> “The booking process feels modern and easy.”

---

# Common Findings

## Positive Feedback
- Clean modern medical UI
- Doctor booking flow is intuitive
- Bottom navigation is simple
- Appointment time selection works well

## Main Confusions
1. Health Score is missing or not visually obvious
2. AI Warning section looks like articles instead of alerts
3. Users expected:
   - health percentage
   - progress charts
   - color indicators
4. Some buttons and dates need larger touch areas

---

# Planned Improvements

## Home Screen
- Add a large Health Score card at the top
- Add circular health progress indicator
- Add color-coded health status:
  - Green = Healthy
  - Yellow = Moderate Risk
  - Red = High Risk

## AI Warning Section
- Add warning icons
- Rename section to:
  “AI Health Alerts”
- Use stronger visual hierarchy

## Doctor Booking
- Add doctor categories
- Add filter/search options
- Increase button visibility

## Accessibility
- Increase font size slightly
- Increase spacing for older users
- Improve touch targets for dates/buttons

---

# Final Conclusion

Most users successfully completed the doctor booking flow without guidance.  
The biggest usability issue was the absence of a clear Health Score section on the Home screen.

The overall design was considered:
- Modern
- Professional
- Easy to navigate
- Suitable for a healthcare mobile application
